Kilometre statistics

Get the kilometres travelled, trip lengths, road types and an estimated travel time from your ANYWAYS report exports, and compare scenarios side by side. Everything is calculated locally in your browser.

Drop the GeoJSON files of your report export(s) here
Per export: the road-segment file (…​.geojson, with count) and optionally the route file (…​_routes.geojson). Several exports at once is fine — they are matched by file name.

Nothing is uploaded. The files stay on your computer.

How is it calculated?

Kilometres on the network = Σ (count × segment length) over all road segments in the export. This is the figure that matches the traffic-volume map in ANYWAYS: each segment counts as many times as there are trips using it. It is also what you get in QGIS with "count" * $length (in a metric CRS).

Route kilometres = the sum of the lengths of all individual routes in the _routes file. This also includes the short stretch from the origin point to the nearest road segment (the connector), and the routes follow a slightly different geometry. This figure is therefore usually a few per cent higher. Use it mainly for trip lengths (mean, median, P85).

Estimated travel time. The export contains no travel times, so the tool estimates them. Per road segment: time = count × length ÷ speed, summed over all segments. The speed depends on the chosen mode of transport:

  • Car: the speed limit of the segment (OSM tag maxspeed) × a factor, 0.7 by default, to allow for junctions, accelerating and braking. Without a usable maxspeed, the zone applies first (zone:traffic, e.g. NL:urban = 50), otherwise a default value per road type (residential street 30, secondary road 50, primary road 70, …).
  • Bicycle: fixed speed, 15 km/h by default.
  • Walking: fixed speed, 5 km/h by default.

Mode of transport. The tool infers the mode of transport itself and shows it as a guess in the selector. It first looks for a profile field in the data (e.g. car.fast), then at the file name (car, bike, walk, …) and finally at the road types used: no footpaths or cycle paths means car, more footpaths or steps than cycle paths means walking, otherwise bicycle. If the guess is wrong, choose yourself; your choice is kept.

Travel-time distribution. The travel time is estimated separately for each route. For cycling and walking, it is the route length ÷ the fixed speed. For the car, each piece of route gets the speed of the segment it lies on; pieces that do not fall on a segment in the export (such as the connector from the origin) use the speed of the previous piece or the average. Because the routes include that connector, the median and P85 are slightly higher than the total travel time would suggest.

The travel time per trip is the total travel time divided by the number of routes. Waiting times at junctions, congestion and the stretch from the origin to the network are not included. It is therefore an approximation, not an output of the ANYWAYS model. Use it mainly to compare scenarios with each other.

Lengths are calculated on the sphere (haversine, WGS84). The difference with a calculation in a local metric CRS is less than 0.5%.
